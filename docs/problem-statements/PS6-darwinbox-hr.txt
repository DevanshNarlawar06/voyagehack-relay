
TBO Hackathon: AI-Native HR Services
Platform(DarwinBox)

The challenge

Design and build an agent-first HR services platform that could replace a conventional
HRMS. Agents do the routine work end to end, humans make the decisions that matter,
and the platform runs at a low, measured cost per task.

Today, an HRMS records events but people still move the work. When a new hire is
added, HR still emails IT for a laptop, Admin for an access card and Finance for payroll
setup, then chases each one. Your platform should close those loops on its own.

You may demonstrate one HR service line in depth, such as talent acquisition,
onboarding, leave, employee helpdesk or exits. The product itself must be a platform
that TBO's in-house developers can extend to new service lines once HR identifies and
approves a need.

Objectives

Your submission must show all eight objectives. Depth on one service line beats
shallow coverage of many.

    1. Automate end to end. Agents trigger and complete cross-team work (IT, Admin,
         Finance, the hiring manager) without manual emails or follow-ups. Report what
         share of your chosen journey's steps finish with no human touch.

    2. Build a platform, not a point solution. A TBO developer must be able to add a
         new service line, agent, workflow or integration without changing the core. Show
         a documented extension model: workflow definitions, an agent or skill registry,
         and API-first integrations.

    3. Keep humans on the decisions that matter. Reserved decisions are routed to a
         named human approver and cannot be completed by an agent. The list of
         reserved decisions is configuration that HR can change, not hard-coded logic
         (see Guardrails).

    4. Comply by design. Legal and policy rules sit in a governed layer that is
         versioned, testable and auditable, not buried in prompts.

    5. Keep a clean system of record. Hold the employee lifecycle from hire to exit in a
         sound data model, with role-based access and a full audit trail of every change
         and who or what made it.

    6. Fail safely. When an agent is unsure, a system is down or data conflicts, the
         work goes to a human queue with context attached. Retries must not duplicate
         actions, for example ordering two laptops.

    7. Serve people where they work. Employees and managers should be able to ask
         and act through a conversational interface (web, Teams or Slack), not only
         through forms.

    8. Prove cost and impact with numbers. Show cost per completed workflow,
         automation rate, cycle time and human-escalation rate on a simple dashboard
         (see Cost and model rule).

Guardrails (non-negotiable)

Automation stops at decisions that carry legal, financial or human consequences. A
submission that lets an agent complete any of these on its own is disqualified from the
top scores.
governed action flow · 7 steps, 1 decision

Every agent action passes a policy check. Reserved or low-confidence actions wait for a
named human, and every outcome is logged.

Reserved for a human (minimum set; HR can add to it):

    · Accepting a resignation, and agreeing a release date or notice-period buyout
    · Final hiring decisions, offer approval and compensation
    · Termination, disciplinary action and performance improvement plans
    · Salary, grade or role changes, and any exception to policy
    · Anything involving POSH, grievance or whistleblower complaints: route only,

         never decide
    · Sign-off on statutory filings and payroll runs

Must be designed in:

    · Data protection: comply with India's DPDP Act, 2023 and its Rules. That means
         purpose limitation, consent where needed, retention limits, and support for
         access and correction requests.

    · PII handling: show exactly what personal data reaches a model and where it is
         hosted. Mask or minimise it before any external model call.

    · Fair hiring: no automated rejection of a candidate without human review.
         Explain why each candidate was ranked where they were.

    · Explainability: every agent action records its inputs, the rule or prompt version,
         the model used and its reasoning.

    · Access control: role-based permissions, so an agent can never do more than
         the person it acts for.

    · Kill switch: HR can pause any agent or workflow instantly without a code
         change.

    · Labour law: statutory obligations (notice periods, final settlement timelines, PF,
         gratuity, leave rules) are encoded as rules, not left to the model to remember.
Cost and model rule

You can use any model from any vendor, but you are judged on measured cost per
completed workflow, so choose the cheapest model that does each job well. HR
services is not a frontier problem. A design that sends routine work to a large frontier
model will score poorly on cost.

    · Measure, don't estimate. Report cost in INR per completed workflow from real
         runs on the synthetic dataset, and project a monthly run cost at TBO's volumes
         (TBO will share assumed headcount and transaction volumes).

    · Use the right tool for each step. Rule-based steps (eligibility checks, date
         calculations, routing) belong in code. Using an LLM where a rule would do
         counts against you.

    · Route between models. Small or open-weight models handle the default path.
         A larger model is used only when a measured confidence or complexity
         threshold calls for it, and you must justify that threshold.

    · Avoid lock-in. Changing the model provider must be a configuration change, not
         a rewrite.

    · Count everything. Include hosting, GPU and inference-provider costs, not only
         token prices. A self-hosted model is not free.

Rules

    · Data: use only the synthetic dataset and mock APIs. Never put real personal
         data into any tool or model.

    · Open source: any library or model you use must have a licence that allows
         commercial use. List each one in your README.

    · AI coding assistants: allowed. Say which ones you used and for what.
    · Individual contribution: keep a clean commit history. Finalists will be asked

         individually about the parts they built.
    · Intellectual property: ownership and licensing of submissions will be set out in

         the participation terms. (TBO to confirm before launch.)
